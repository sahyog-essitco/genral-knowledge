## Notes for Using AWG Table

1. **Wire Availability**
   - **Very High:** Commonly stocked at local hardware or electrical stores (14 AWG, 12 AWG, 16 AWG, 20 AWG).  
   - **High:** Found at specialty electrical suppliers or online (10 AWG, 18 AWG).  
   - **Moderate:** Usually only online or electronics suppliers (22 AWG, 24 AWG).  

2. **Nearest Upper AWG Selection**
   - If your exact AWG is unavailable, **always choose the next thicker wire**.  
   - Example: Need 18 AWG but only 16 AWG is available → use 16 AWG.  

3. **Current Capacity**
   - Values assume copper wire at typical ambient temperature.  
   - **Short runs (<0.5 m / ~1.5 ft):** Voltage drop is negligible; focus on current rating.  
   - For longer runs, consider voltage drop and select a thicker wire if needed.  

4. **Safety Margin**
   - If your load is at or near a wire’s max rating, use the **next thicker wire** for safety.  
   - Example: 30 A load at 24 V → 10 AWG works for <0.5 m, 8 AWG gives extra margin.  

5. **Voltage Rating**
   - Determined by insulation type.  
   - Small electronics wires: ~300 V.  
   - Household/industrial wires: up to 600 V.  

6. **Typical Applications**
   - Low-voltage electronics (<24 V, <10 A): 24–18 AWG.  
   - Household circuits (120 V): 14–12 AWG for 15–20 A, 10 AWG for 30 A.  
   - Industrial equipment (240 V, 20–50 A): 10–6 AWG depending on load.
